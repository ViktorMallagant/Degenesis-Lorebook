# Balkhan

## The Wild Lands

Balkhan stretches across mountains, forests, fertile plateaus, and the remains of cities repeatedly claimed and abandoned. Storms sweep its plains, rivers swell through its valleys, and winter can bury entire mountain forests beneath deep snow. Among these extremes, communities preserve a fierce attachment to their own people and an equally fierce determination to govern themselves. Hospitality, competition, family loyalty, and pride shape a Culture whose strength seldom belongs to a single ruler for long.

Across the region, survival depends upon relationships as much as terrain. A shared table can establish trust, an insult can reopen a feud, and an external threat can unite enemies who would otherwise refuse one another’s company. Those bonds have helped Balkhan resist conquest. They also make its political life unstable, as alliances forged in danger fracture when the immediate enemy retreats.

## A Land of Extremes

Heavy rain sends torrents down the mountains, joining streams into dangerous currents. Forests grip slopes and shelter communities, while open grasslands endure the heat of summer. In winter, cold and deep snow restrict movement and isolate settlements. Travelers must understand the season, the route, and the people controlling the next crossing before treating any journey as straightforward.

Fertile regions nevertheless support substantial settlements. Around Sofia, mountain springs feed rivers and productive farmland, sustaining a city capable of extending roads, defenses, and authority into its surroundings. Elsewhere, trees reclaim streets and break apart Bygone buildings. Balkhan’s abundance offers opportunities for cultivation, hunting, and reconstruction, but access to those resources depends upon local claims and the willingness to defend them.

## Hospitality, Kinship, and Honor

A welcome carries real weight in Balkhani life. Food, drink, celebration, and the recognition of a guest create relationships through which strangers can become companions. Strength and spirited competition earn admiration, while family and Clan provide a framework for belonging. Personal conduct matters because generosity, courage, and insult can affect the standing of everyone associated with a person.

These loyalties can accommodate both bitter conflict and reconciliation. Relatives may quarrel violently and still stand together against an outsider; neighboring Clans may suspend generations of hostility when their homeland is threatened. Such solidarity does not erase differences within the Culture. It gives those differences a particular intensity, tying political disagreements to relationships that people experience as personal obligations.

## The Voivodes and Their Clans

Voivodates organize much of Balkhan’s political power around warlords, their followers, and the territories they can hold. Cities such as Beograd and Sofia concentrate troops, supplies, and influence, while Boyars and other local powers support, contest, or seek to replace the rulers above them. A fortress can survive successive dynasties even as its banners and loyalties change.

Authority remains closely connected to the ruler’s ability to reward service and punish opposition. Titles, privileges, protection, and access to trade can secure followers, but coercion and suspicion can rapidly undermine those relationships. Ambition within a Voivodate is therefore inseparable from the question of who will stand with whom when the next struggle begins.

## Resistance to the African Advance

African conquest brought Scourgers, Surge Tanks, and Neolibyan administration into territories whose inhabitants refused to accept defeat as submission. Villages were attacked and captives taken into slavery, while the invaders sought routes through the region’s difficult terrain. Mountains and forests gave the resistance places to hide, observe, and strike without confronting every enemy advantage directly.

Rival Clans joined forces against the advance. Scouts tracked troop movements, raiders disrupted camps, and seemingly useful alliances became opportunities for betrayal. Resistance continued among enslaved Balkhani taken abroad, making them dangerous captives as well as determined opponents at home. The struggle demonstrated what the Culture could achieve through shared purpose, without establishing a lasting peace among its own communities.

## A Victory That Divides

One decisive blow against the invasion came through Voivode Neven’s apparent cooperation with the Neolibyan Zuberi. Offered cavalry and assistance against Bucharest, the invading army followed routes that ultimately led to a prepared resting place. A celebration concealed the trap: Neven sacrificed himself to destroy a Surge Tank, setting off the buried explosives that devastated the army. The loss helped bring the Bank of Commerce’s campaign of conquest to a halt.

Foreign involvement also ran beneath that victory. Supplies had crossed from Borca under impeccable papers, and the Chronicler Fragment Impulse observed the destruction through a drone, defending a homeland bound to his own ancestry. Liberation could therefore depend upon outside connections even as Balkhani leaders condemned outside influence. Once the Africans withdrew, shared anger increasingly turned toward the Cults living and working within Balkhan itself.

## Cults Under Clan Rule

After the war, many Clans accused the Cults of failing them. Jehammedans maintained communities of their own, Spitalians entered the region and confronted the Dushani, Apocalyptics pursued their interests, and Hellvetic withdrawal behind the Fortress became another source of resentment. Earlier grievances acquired fresh force in the aftermath of collective sacrifice.

Across much of Balkhan, Cult representatives must justify their activities to the Voivodes. Permission depends upon local authority rather than the prestige an organization enjoys elsewhere, and defiance can end in enslavement. For people whose Culture and Cult demand different loyalties, this creates an immediate difficulty: service to a wider institution may be treated as a betrayal of the homeland that raised them.

## Usud and the Dushani

Beyond the Carpathians lies the Usud Crater, its dark lake surrounded by Mother spore fields and forests marked by Sepsis. Across these landscapes, the Dushani work through sound and resonance. Wind, stone, water, trees, and caverns become parts of an environment they tune, altering the flow of water or the placement of natural features until the surroundings answer their voices.

Their influence reaches into human feeling and the physical world. Chants can evoke comfort or pain, while concentrated resonance turns familiar terrain into something dangerous and profoundly altered. The Dushani’s relationship with nature gives Balkhan’s forests and mountains another layer of uncertainty. A place that offers shelter from soldiers may also belong to forces whose needs and perceptions are very different from those of its human inhabitants.

## The Singing Ruins

Laibach stands where Balkhan meets Purgare, on a route used by smugglers. Its concrete buildings resist the forest’s reclamation, while strange acoustic phenomena linger through streets and walls. Balkhani explanations connect the voices to the city’s history or to souls taken by the Dushani Mokosch, Eidolon. Whatever their origin, the sounds accompany more tangible dangers, including a bandaged figure whose deadly attacks make lingering in the ruins perilous.

Pest embodies the Dushani’s power on a far greater scale. Ruins, waterways, hills, and spore fields form an immense system of resonance in which a disturbance can draw a lethal response. Even Apocalyptics entering for Burn must learn extraordinary caution. Chernobog’s advance challenges that system directly, sacrificing followers to break a route through the city as Dushani gather to reinforce it. Human conquest here collides with a landscape organized around an alien purpose.

## Beneath the Mountains

Ancient tunnels, flooded passages, sealed doors, and later bunker complexes extend beneath large areas of Balkhan. Before the Eshaton, the Recombination Group expanded this underground network through Project Tannhäuser, moving people and supplies into the Carpathians. Centuries later, openings in that buried world expose communities whose lives developed far from the surface.

Paler descendants of the former guards preserve inherited rituals and await the awakening of their Sleepers. Failing supplies increasingly drive them above ground, where theft and raids bring them into conflict with Balkhani settlements. For Scrappers and rulers, the bunkers also promise valuable technology. For the people already living within them, intrusion threatens a home and a sacred inheritance, making the underground another contested part of the Culture.

## Beograd and the Twin Tower

Beograd’s Twin Tower has endured sieges, fires, fortification, and changes of dynasty. Around it, markets, homes, inns, arsenals, and walls give a warlord’s stronghold the life of a substantial city. Under the Djuric dynasty, its continuing presence offers a symbol of authority even as the ruler faces enemies within his territory and beyond it.

Suspicion shapes the current Voivode’s response to crisis. After an epidemic, Spitalians who had fought the disease were accused of poisoning the population and sold into slavery. Apocalyptics were confined to a controlled quarter, while threatening Usudi, Dalmatian attacks, Sofia’s preparations, and Chernobog’s approaching Clans deepen the pressure. Djuric relies on body doubles and trusted confederations, often recruited from Pollen, revealing how little security political strength can provide when loyalty remains uncertain.

## Sofia and Karakhan

Sofia draws strength from a fertile plateau sheltered by mountains and guarded passes. Fortifications control the approaches, while roads connect agricultural settlements and watchtowers to the city. Its palaces, metal-clad buildings, and great halls express an ambition to organize the surrounding land rather than simply defend an isolated stronghold.

Karakhan reinforces that ambition through personal attention to soldiers, tax collectors, messengers, and reconstruction. Recovered weapons and armor equip his officers, while expeditions into Paler bunkers expand his resources through violence. At his side stands an African woman whose identity and relationship to him remain uncertain to observers. Their partnership complicates the easy division between Balkhani independence and African influence, just as Sofia’s growing discipline challenges the assumption that all local power must remain fragmented.

## Bucharest and Divided Faith

Bucharest bears the marks of floods, collapsed towers, burned districts, and repeated occupation. Jehammedan settlement brought renewed life through preaching, herds, and established communities, but also involved conquest and the suppression of opposing tribes. The city’s religious importance and material wealth made it a prize for neighboring powers.

Wars against Anabaptists and Africans exhausted its defenders before Karakhan’s army arrived. Surrender brought another rupture when the Isaaki Arioch killed the Iconide who had yielded the city. Among families who had lost their sons, the protection of surviving daughters became an urgent concern. Karakhan offered security to Jehammedan women in exchange for permanent allegiance, binding the survival of vulnerable households to the authority of a new ruler.

## Dalmatia and the Uses of Freedom

An old arrangement between the Zlatan dynasty and Dalmatia’s Jehammedan Iconides divided authority and territory between native communities and the Cult. Generations maintained an uneasy coexistence without overcoming their mutual distrust. Buzdovan ended that balance by murdering the aging Voivode and seizing power under the promise of freedom.

His rule turned liberation into persecution. Jehammedans were outlawed and enslaved, rival Boyars eliminated, and other Cults exposed to violence according to his shifting demands. Looted Icons entered markets alongside imitations, while new ceremonies attempted to give his reign legitimacy. Beneath the display of confidence lies fear of a relic said to foretell his defeat. Dalmatia shows how pride in independence can be used to justify authority as brutal as any foreign occupation.

## Turkey and the Advancing Green

Along the former Turkish coast, Psychovore seeds carried through Mediterranean waters take root and spread into forests, wetlands, and settlements. The growing plant front displaces animals and drives people from their homes, adding an expanding environmental danger to a region already shaped by conquest.

Stories tell of surviving Turkish communities within sealed enclaves, whose protective suits and substances allow them to move through the hostile vegetation. Outsiders cannot assume those accounts offer a readily available solution. Neolibyans seek the means of protection, while a joint expedition with Spitalians studies the Psychovores and prepares an experiment. As elsewhere in Balkhan, local knowledge attracts institutions whose interest can bring both assistance and intrusion.

## The Balkhani Conflict

Balkhan’s communities possess the loyalty, resourcefulness, and determination to resist powerful enemies. Fertile land, fortified cities, forests, and buried technology offer foundations upon which to build enduring strength. Yet the same relationships that sustain resistance can carry old grievances into every new alliance, while leaders invoke freedom to demand obedience from the people beneath them.

A Balkhani character may carry obligations to family, Clan, Voivode, and Cult that cannot all be satisfied at once. Hospitality can create a friendship that political demands threaten; pride in resisting conquest can clash with disgust at local persecution; loyalty to a ruler can falter when protection becomes punishment. Across the Culture, the struggle is to preserve belonging and independence while deciding how far loyalty should extend, what authority deserves support, and whom freedom must protect.
