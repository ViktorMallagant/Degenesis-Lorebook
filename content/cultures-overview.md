# Cultures

## Seven Cultures, One Broken World

By 2595, more than five centuries have passed since the Eshaton shattered the old world. Across Europe and Africa, survivors have built new societies among ruined cities, altered coastlines, encroaching glaciers, and landscapes transformed by the Primer. Seven Cultures define the world presented in Degenesis: Africa, Borca, Balkhan, Franka, Hybrispania, Pollen, and Purgare. Each carries its own memories of catastrophe, its own understanding of belonging, and its own answer to the question of how humanity should endure.

Culture grows from the relationship between people and their homeland. Climate, resources, neighboring communities, inherited customs, and generations of conflict shape what people value and what they fear. A salvaged machine, a family obligation, an ancestral shrine, or a shared meal can carry a different meaning on opposite sides of a border. Those differences influence trade and diplomacy as readily as they influence daily life.

Within every Culture, cities, villages, Clans, and families preserve distinct ways of living. Shared roots can unite people who disagree bitterly about religion, authority, or the future. A Culture offers a broad sense of identity; individual communities and personal experience give that identity its particular shape.

## A Land Remade

Across the map, the Eshaton’s legacy remains impossible to ignore. The Reaper’s Blow tears through the continent, glaciers press into the north, and the retreat of the seas has exposed new routes and fertile ground. Elsewhere, dust, volcanic activity, contaminated soil, and hostile vegetation turn familiar geography into a struggle for survival. Ruins still mark the presence of the Bygones, but reaching them, understanding them, and claiming their contents are very different tasks.

These conditions help explain the Cultures’ differences. Communities surrounded by recoverable machinery develop different priorities from those that must abandon their fields and move on. Fertile plains encourage settlement, while mountain passes and protected harbors become sources of power. A defensible city can promise security and demand obedience in return. Geography shapes opportunity, and the institutions controlling that opportunity help determine how people live.

## Roots and Allegiances

Culture and Cult meet throughout everyday life. Cultures give people a homeland and a framework of customs; Cults organize knowledge, belief, labor, and power. Their influence crosses regional boundaries, bringing distant communities into contact while also creating friction with local traditions. Membership in the same Cult can connect people from different Cultures, while allegiance to different Cults can divide neighbors who share the same birthplace.

Across these relationships, identity becomes complicated. Loyalty to family can conflict with a Cult’s demands. A village may welcome the medicine, weapons, or trade offered by outsiders while resenting the authority that accompanies them. For travelers and characters alike, understanding a place requires attention to both its cultural roots and the organizations competing to shape its future.

## The Seven Cultures

### Africa

Africa’s flourishing cities, expansive trade, and ancestral traditions express a confidence that much of Europe struggles to match. Under the symbol of the Lion, Neolibyans, Scourgers, and Anubians embody complementary roles in commerce, protection, and spiritual life. Yet prosperity coexists with debt, slavery, and resistance to commercial authority. Beyond the ports and plantations, the advancing Psychovores threaten settlements and unsettle the relationship between ancestral belief and an increasingly alien landscape.

### Borca

Borca builds upon the physical remains of the Bygone world. Scrappers search ruined cities for useful machinery and valuable artifacts, while growing settlements turn recovered metal and knowledge into new industry. Justitian and its Protectorate offer law and protection through the Judges, supported and contested by other powerful Cults. Beyond their walls, independent Clans defend their own claims. Borcan reconstruction therefore carries a persistent question: who benefits from civilization, and who must surrender to it?

### Balkhan

Forests, mountains, and fierce seasonal extremes frame a Culture shaped by hospitality, pride, family ties, and volatile alliances. Voivodes and their Clans exercise power locally, and outsiders must navigate obligations that can matter more than distant institutions. Shared enemies can draw rival communities together, but victory seldom erases old grievances. Among the wild landscapes, the Dushani add another source of danger and uncertainty, binding the region’s struggle for independence to forces beyond ordinary human politics.

### Franka

Across much of Franka, the Pheromancers’ influence reaches through insect swarms, altered environments, and the emotions of human communities. Freedom depends upon maintaining the settlements and routes that remain beyond their control. Anabaptists, Spitalians, local Clans, and other allies resist that advance, while trade and recovered knowledge sustain the free regions. Frankan life turns upon the difficult task of preserving agency, community, and cultural memory under a threat that can make submission feel like belonging.

### Hybrispania

Hybrispania lives with the consequences of an invasion of Africa that returned as conquest upon its own soil. Guerreros fight through humid forests and fortified territories, preserving memories of resistance and sacrifice while opposing African domination. Survival demands mobility, secrecy, local knowledge, and uneasy cooperation. Meanwhile, disturbances of time complicate a land already burdened by inherited violence. Personal loyalties and the struggle for liberation can pull in different directions, especially when yesterday’s grievance becomes tomorrow’s duty.

### Pollen

Across Pollen, survival often means knowing when to leave. Nomadic communities carry their belongings between oases and defend temporary refuges against cold, scarcity, and the dangers of the wasteland. Strength and mutual dependence sustain them while Sepsis, Biokinetics, and the extraordinary growth of the Fractal Forests transform the ground beneath their feet. Burn offers relief from hardship while deepening the threat of infection. Amid these competing pressures, the ability to protect a community matters more than any promise of permanence.

### Purgare

Purgare combines poisoned, volcanic devastation with fertile land capable of supporting communities and armies. Family reputation, inherited obligations, omens, and faith shape its social world, while Anabaptist influence gives many communities a shared religious purpose. The Psychokinetics make the surrounding dangers tangible and unpredictable. Between productive fields and hostile landscapes, Purgans must preserve the people and traditions that sustain them while deciding whose authority deserves their trust.

## Across the Borders

Caravans, ships, mercenaries, pilgrims, and refugees connect these Cultures. Goods travel with stories, customs, prejudices, and ambitions. A route that brings prosperity can also carry an occupying force; an ally against one enemy can become a rival over land or resources. Across the Mediterranean and the mountain passes, cooperation and exploitation frequently share the same road.

Every Culture contains both achievements worth preserving and conflicts capable of undoing them. Their people build homes, raise families, celebrate, trade, and hope even when surrounded by danger. For characters moving between them, these differences offer more than unfamiliar scenery: they create relationships, obligations, and choices that can challenge everything a person has learned to take for granted.

## Mechanical Summary

Choose one of the seven Cultures as your character’s origin. This choice establishes the homeland and people that have most strongly shaped their early life. Think about the community within that Culture where they grew up: a prosperous city, a farming village, a wandering Clan, an isolated settlement, or another place that fits the setting. Decide which customs they keep, which expectations they question, and what they still consider home. These details give you something concrete to bring into play.

Your Culture also determines which Attribute and Skill maximums receive a **+1 MAX** increase during character creation. These increases let you spend more of your available points on the listed traits; they do not grant free Attribute or Skill points. You can use the higher limits where they suit your character and leave them unused elsewhere. Consult the individual Culture’s bonus boxes when allocating points.

The Common Cults listed for a Culture describe the affiliations usually encountered there. Other combinations remain possible, provided your background explains them convincingly. Consider how your character encountered that Cult, why it accepted them, and what ties remain to their homeland. An unusual combination can be especially useful when it creates relationships and obligations that matter during play.

Together, Culture, Concept, and Cult give you a starting point for a person with roots, motives, and commitments. Let Culture inform your character’s habits and perspective while leaving room for experience to change them. What they choose to preserve, abandon, or become belongs to the story you create at the table.
