# Pollen

## Eternal Wanderings

Across Pollen, survival depends upon moving before the land takes back what it has offered. Clans carry their possessions on sleds between scattered refuges, searching for food, warmth, and ground that will remain habitable long enough to sustain them. Cold, red dust, and Sepsis make every journey demanding, while the people traveling together provide a continuity that no settlement can guarantee.

Beneath these wanderings, two forms of the Primer struggle over the landscape. Mother spore fields spread through ruined country, while Fractal Forests replace infected ground with extraordinary growth. Both can destroy human lives, yet the forests also shelter communities that would otherwise face hunger and exposure. Pollen’s people endure between powers they cannot fully control, defending temporary homes and deciding when attachment must give way to departure.

## Uprooted by the Eshaton

Before the impact, predictions identified the country around and east of Warsaw as a certain disaster zone. Organized evacuations carried people toward the Sudetes and High Tatra, where emergency settlements promised protection among mountains and forests. The Eshaton nevertheless devastated much of Poland, spreading destruction and red dust far beyond the point of impact. Survival in the mountains preserved people without preserving the homeland they remembered.

Reports of intact woodland and recoverable stores eventually drew families back into the ruins. They searched for supplies, uncovered fields, planted crops, and traveled between cultivation and salvage as the seasons changed. Some communities established a workable rhythm, but scarcity left others outside it. Hunger transformed displaced survivors into raiders, forcing the emerging Clans to protect both their harvests and the people whose labor had made those harvests possible.

## Community and Strength

Pollner life places considerable value upon strength because a community’s survival depends upon what its members can carry, defend, and endure. Refugees arriving from the east reinforced that outlook. Led by imposing elders whose authority had survived a long and violent journey, they offered an example of leadership rooted in power and obedience. Their acceptance into Pollen helped strengthen traditions of dominance within societies already living under severe pressure.

Yet communal life requires knowledge and trust as well as force. People must recognize dangerous terrain, prepare unfamiliar food, and cooperate when a refuge fails. Sharing a meal can therefore become a consequential invitation rather than casual hospitality. A stranger’s caution may be understood without earning continued cooperation. Within a Culture where mistakes can kill an entire group, relationships grow through confidence in the people whose judgment keeps that group alive.

## Burn and the Burden of Survival

Burn has become part of ordinary life for many Pollners. Its effects make cold, hunger, and illness feel more bearable, offering relief in a country where comfort is scarce. That relief carries the danger of deeper infection, but immediate hardship can make distant consequences difficult to weigh. Reliance upon the substance binds human survival to the spore fields producing it and helps sustain one of Europe’s most profitable drug markets.

Apocalyptic trade draws heavily upon Pollen’s abundant Bion Burn. As Fractal Forests replace productive spore fields, harvesting moves farther east and encounters increasingly aggressive Biokinetics. Traders blame Spitalian and Anabaptist intervention for the loss of their livelihood. Some defend the fields, attack doctors, or burn the forests sustaining local Clans. The economy of relief thus becomes a struggle over which dangerous form of the landscape should be allowed to survive.

## Biokinetics and the Discordance

Among the early Clans, altered children initially appeared to embody adaptation. Resistance to cold, rapid healing, and extraordinary growth suggested that the next generation might be better suited to its damaged homeland. Increasing bodily changes soon made that hope difficult to sustain. Children known as Leechers eventually abandoned their communities for the spore fields, where they developed into Biokinetics and became participants in the Primer’s expanding collective.

The Discordance shattered that first generation’s connection to the Chakra network when it encountered the Psychovores’ collective. Many Biokinetics lost themselves in the collapse and degenerated into Spore Beasts that still haunt Pollen. Later generations restored the threat, spreading Sepsis and defending its fields. Around their nests, heavily infected Pandorians attack intruders with an allegiance that places them beyond the ordinary relationships connecting human Clans.

## The Fractal Forests

Fractal Forests emerge with startling speed. Ground rises and trembles, spore walls crumble, and translucent vegetation grows across places previously dominated by Sepsis. Trees harden, leaves unfold, and branching arrangements repeat in spirals, circles, and other elaborate patterns. Warm earth, damp air, and abundant growth create the appearance of a refuge where the surrounding wastes offer little hope.

That appearance conceals an unfamiliar form of life. Structures resembling small animals can be parts of the plants themselves, while vigorous roots and shoots remain dangerous even after harvesting. The forests oppose the spore fields, but their success does not make them ordinary woodland or demonstrate that they exist for humanity’s benefit. Their fertility gives Pollners opportunities that demand close observation, inherited precautions, and a willingness to leave when the living system begins to fail.

## Living with the Forests

Clans shelter within the Fractal Forests, collecting roots and bulbs and using hot springs against the cold. Food must be cooked for hours before it can be eaten safely. Raw growth can take root inside the body and spread with lethal speed, turning an apparent meal into an invasion. Knowledge of preparation makes abundance usable and gives experienced members of the community an authority that physical strength alone cannot replace.

Fire introduces another danger. Burning growth can trigger rapid hardening into crystalline thorns and draw life from neighboring roots, potentially destroying the refuge. Campfires therefore belong along the forest’s edge. In return for shelter, Clanners remove webs, kill Rift Centipedes, and defend the trees against people who would destroy them. Their home becomes a relationship maintained through daily labor, although its requirements can extend far beyond that labor.

## The Price of Abundance

Within some forests, the earth opens to reveal large fleshy chambers known as phagocyte cusps. Clans feed them animals, but offerings can also include enemies, captured Spitalians, or children regarded as blessed. The chamber closes around its victim and disappears beneath renewed growth. A community’s dependence upon the forest can therefore turn nourishment and protection into justification for the sacrifice of human lives.

Eventually, a forest exhausts its vitality. Trees break apart, fruit decays, and vegetation dissolves, prompting its inhabitants to give thanks and move on. Afterward, the chambers reopen. Animals emerge unharmed, while human offerings return without having aged during their confinement. They have nevertheless changed. The process makes the forests’ generosity inseparable from a purpose that the departing Clans do not necessarily understand.

## A Contested Paradise

Spitalians regard the Fractal Forests as another expression of the threat they fight, while some Anabaptists see a possible revelation. Wetzel the Chosen’s encounter near Lodz helped inspire belief that the forests descend from the tree of knowledge. The fruit remains perilous: visions can overwhelm those approaching it, and accounts of revelation include madness. High-ranking Baptists’ willingness to consume it places religious authority beside knowledge that remains closely guarded.

Chroniclers pursue a different possibility. Changes to the phagocyte cusps appear to alter the forests’ growth patterns, suggesting that these chambers provide access to the system organizing the whole. Whether the forests might function as immense biological computers remains a question rather than an established explanation. Across these interpretations, Pollner communities defend places others approach as enemies, sacred mysteries, or opportunities for investigation.

## The Eternal Oases

Fractal Forests are comparatively recent discoveries, but Pollen’s stories also describe older refuges known as Eternal Oases. Hidden near rivers or behind distant cliffs, these places support familiar vegetation despite the cold. Their reputed fertility differs from the violent growth replacing spore fields. For generations, knowledge of such sites has represented the possibility of dependable sustenance in a country where dependable sustenance grants enormous power.

Their locations remain closely protected, leaving outsiders to work with rumor. The Piast of Wroclaw is said to control three oases, an explanation offered for the city’s prosperity through years of devastation. The claim is unconfirmed, but its importance is clear. Where most communities must keep moving, control of a lasting source of food could sustain authority, support a city, and determine who is permitted to share in its security.

## Pandora and the Silk Trade

The Pandora Crater stands at the center of Pollen’s devastation. Its immense rim is marked by spore walls, and water carries unfamiliar organisms from its slopes into ancient channels. Across the surrounding landscape, webs conceal ruins, bind structures, and cover ravines that can collapse beneath travelers. Windborne spiders are often harmless in themselves, yet their silk and bodies spread contamination across the places people must cross.

Eastern Przadkas turn that dangerous abundance into valuable material. Their women gather silk, then wash, heat, comb, and spin it for Wroclaw’s mills. Finished fabric reaches African markets through Neolibyan trade and also protects the city’s Druschinnik guards. Its durability gives it value in both commerce and defense. Pollen’s people build an economy from the organisms threatening them, using practical skill to recover something useful without making the source safe.

## Wroclaw and the Piast

Wroclaw offers a striking concentration of wealth and order. African visitors pursue Biokinetics as trophies, hiring guides and beaters while buying provisions, entertainment, and unusual goods. Neolibyan Surge Tanks contribute to its impressive defenses, and local merchants benefit from visitors whose money reaches beyond ordinary subsistence. The city demonstrates that Pollen can support commerce and specialized work alongside nomadic survival.

Its stability remains guarded by secrecy. Twelve districts stand behind walls and gates, while strangers are confined to the merchants’ quarter. The Piast’s orders reach the population through the Druschinniks, who alone have access to their ruler. Legends describe a leader surviving for centuries, although outsiders suspect a concealed succession. The sources of the city’s food and the circumstances within its closed districts remain uncertain, even as the Piast rejects Spitalian offers to tend its sick.

## Roads, Ruins, and Brno

Hellvetic passages connect Pollen to the Alpine routes, but emerging from a protected tunnel does not make the onward journey secure. Sulfurous winds cross exposed country, and shelters may already be occupied by animals or Apocalyptic stores. Routes toward Wroclaw pass territory affected by the destruction of Praha Republika. Dresden’s ruined streets attract stories of ghosts, surviving Archivists, and violent inhabitants; travelers lack a safe reason to dismiss every warning.

Brno remains divided after Chernobog’s passage. Clans that stayed behind compete for succession, while salvage from Praha brings books, artifacts, and weapons into local markets. Spitalians attempt agreements with leaders whose positions change rapidly, and Hellvetics defend the passage road through fortifications and force. The city’s access to important routes coexists with unstable authority, making commerce possible without resolving the violence surrounding it.

## Danzig and the Ice Barrier

Danzig is gradually disappearing beneath advancing ice. Heating towers sustain the Spitalian hospital, but its doctors have already retreated from the upper floors into basement laboratories. Consultant Dr. Janssen’s research uses living Biokinetics to investigate whether extreme conditions can force the Primer beyond a host’s capacity to adapt. The experiments reveal extraordinary biological persistence, while broken supply routes and Spore Beasts in the tunnels threaten the researchers themselves.

Anabaptists maintain a separate foothold in St. Mary the Cold, rebuilding the church as an expression of their refusal to surrender even frozen ground to the Demiurge. Nearby, Garganti travel the wastes on mammoths, demonstrating that the Ice Barrier is not empty of human life. Their mobility contrasts with institutions holding buildings against the cold. Both approaches demand resolve, but they embody different answers to when endurance requires remaining and when it requires movement.

## At the Sporefront

Eastward, Pollen’s Sepsis has joined the infected regions spreading from the Balkhan, forming a barrier that separates the known European world from Asia. Near Brest, Spitalians have spent decades attempting to maintain a corridor through it. Chronicler and Spitalian technology combine in Destruction Fortresses that release fungicides into the surrounding ground, keeping routes open at a cost that makes unprotected passage impossible.

The corridor continually narrows beneath renewed growth and must be forced open again. Dr. Glukhovsky’s expedition reached beyond the barrier six years before 2595, but a Biokinetic assault broke the positions and supply lines behind it. No reliable report has established the expedition’s fate. The Sporefront therefore marks both a military struggle and a limit upon knowledge, with hope of contact beyond it persisting alongside the expectation that nothing will return.

## Ruins and Inherited Fears

Pollen’s changing ground sometimes returns Bygone cities to view. Buildings rise through webs and buried layers as the land shifts, offering Scrappers access to places long concealed. Such openings can disappear again, making salvage dependent upon opportunity and timing. The same movement that threatens a camp can uncover valuable remnants of history, drawing outsiders into territory where their maps and assumptions provide little protection.

Legends preserve another form of inheritance. Stories of the Wretched Hag describe an ancient, ravenous woman haunting the ruins and awaiting judgment upon those who abandoned her. Parents use the tale to warn children, while early records suggest that its roots reach close to the Eshaton itself. The figure’s true nature remains uncertain. Her endurance in collective memory shows how fear can become part of a people’s shared history long after its original cause is obscured.

## The Pollner Conflict

A Pollner character grows from a world where community can remain dependable while the ground itself changes. They may trust the companions who taught them to prepare dangerous food, remember the refuge their Clan was forced to abandon, or come from a city whose security depends upon keeping outsiders ignorant. Strength matters, but so do patience, observation, and the ability to recognize when defending a place will cost the people it was meant to shelter.

Pollen’s central struggle concerns what survival asks a person to accept. Burn offers relief while deepening dependence upon Sepsis. Forests provide warmth and food while demanding disturbing sacrifices. Powerful rulers can sustain prosperity without sharing knowledge or access. For a Pollner, loyalty becomes meaningful through deciding whom to protect, which customs still deserve obedience, and what they will carry forward when the time comes to move again.
