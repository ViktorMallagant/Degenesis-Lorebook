# Purgare

## Land of the Chosen

Purgare sustains its people between fertile fields and a landscape scarred by volcanic destruction. Families cultivate vineyards and grain, defend inherited homes, and carry reputations built over generations. Across the Apennines, poisonous air and the impossible workings of the Psychokinetics make survival depend upon shelter, vigilance, and obligations that can be stronger than any distant law.

Anabaptist faith has given much of the Culture a shared purpose, interpreting its fertile eastern lands as evidence of Paradise and its western horrors as the battlefield of humanity’s destiny. Yet Purgans do not agree upon who should lead that struggle. Patriarchs preserve their authority, independent families resist religious intervention, and communities beyond the mountains find ways to live with dangers others seek to destroy.

## A Homeland Divided

The Eshaton drove an asteroid into the ground east of Vesuvius, opening volcanic chambers and devastating the surrounding land. Along the western coast, the Reaper’s Blow continues to release heat, basalt, and poisonous vapors. Corrosive air and contaminated water erode the remains of cities, while foul material accumulates upon shores that once supported leisure and commerce. The destruction remains part of the present rather than a catastrophe confined to memory.

The Apennines interrupt the movement of these vapors, dividing Purgare into regions with different prospects. Eastern fields support settlements and armies, while much of the west demands constant adaptation to environmental danger. The mountains can be crossed, but crossing changes the conditions under which a person must live. Some travel west to seek battle, others to escape disgrace or outside authority, carrying their histories into a country where familiar protections may fail.

## Family as Law

Family gives a Purgan identity, standing, and a place within the world. Traditions establish duties, regulate conduct, and determine how achievements or offenses affect everyone sharing a name. The patriarch combines affection with judgment, rewarding service through responsibility and opportunities while punishing conduct that damages the family’s reputation. Personal success matters because it strengthens a lineage whose claims extend beyond any individual life.

These bonds shape relationships with outsiders. A person without family support can find their reliability questioned before negotiations begin, while marriage joins questions of affection to the standing and security of entire Clans. Protection carries expectations of obedience, and the same institution that offers belonging can restrict a person’s future. Purgan authority therefore grows from intimate relationships, making disagreements about duty difficult to separate from loyalty to the people who raised someone.

## Signs and Wonders

Psychokinetic phenomena give Purgan beliefs an unsettling foundation. Rifts, distorted light, floating debris, and invisible forces can injure or kill without behaving like ordinary hazards. Spitalians seek explanations through research and dissection, while many inhabitants understand the surrounding world through omens, sacred places, and the possibility of miracles. Experience supplies ample reason to take unseen dangers seriously, even when explanations differ.

Augurs read birds, sacrificial remains, and the sky for guidance about harvests and journeys. Other holy figures enter the vapors of the Reaper’s Blow or practice severe bodily penance, gaining respect through acts outsiders may regard with alarm. Such traditions give communities ways to interpret uncertainty and suffering. Anabaptist teachings have spread through this existing religious landscape, replacing some customs while adapting the desire for meaning and protection to their own vision of the world.

## Elysian Oils and Anabaptist Influence

Anabaptist influence reaches Purgare through cultivation, military commitment, religious persuasion, and Elysian oils. Ascetics work beside farmers, while Emissaries approach leading families with assistance and promises of renewal. The oils can relieve pain and restore vigor, making their effects persuasive where sermons and appeals to duty have failed. Relief experienced within a patriarch’s own body can reshape the decisions of an entire household.

The Catalanos illustrate that change. Long resistance to Anabaptist involvement weakened when an Elysian’s treatment offered their aging patriarch renewed strength. Family banners and inherited symbols gradually acquired the broken cross as more households accepted the Cult. That acceptance brings practical benefits and shared purpose, but also dependence. Families must decide how much authority they will grant the people supplying protection, labor, and preparations they increasingly value.

## The Adriatic Frontier

The retreat of the Adriatic exposed fertile lowlands where a river carries clear glacial water toward the Mediterranean. Anabaptist Emissaries interpreted this river as one of Paradise’s surviving waterways, making its defense part of a sacred undertaking. Purgan farmers and settlers from the Balkhan nevertheless shared claims to the same productive ground. Competition over fields became violence, and violence hardened into a frontier maintained for centuries.

Fortified camps, damaged boats, and ground burned by incendiary attacks preserve the conflict’s accumulated cost. Broken crosses face Balkhani ram symbols across the river, joining territorial rivalry to inherited accounts of sacrifice and victory. By 2595, exchanges between Anabaptists and Voivodes have interrupted the familiar pattern of attack. Gifts pass between boats meeting upon the water, but neither side has forgotten the positions from which it fought. Peace remains possible without becoming secure.

## Bergamo and Veneto

Bergamo expresses a form of Purgan independence that Cathedral City has failed to absorb. The Lombardi settled its surviving hill city, benefited from nearby Hellvetic passage routes, and built prosperity through trade with Chronicler assistance. Disgraced or deserting Hellvetics strengthened the family’s ranks. Repeated Anabaptist offers were rejected, and sheltering a Jehammedan Clan deepened the breach. Cathedral City now prohibits its members from entering, leaving the Lombardi’s success outside its religious order.

In Veneto, the Catalanos preside over grain fields, extensive kinship ties, and increasingly Anabaptist customs. Family rivalries narrow their choices of marriage and alliance, while agricultural wealth makes their decisions consequential across Purgare. Venice belongs to their domain but remains surrounded by fear. Stories describe an ancient presence beneath its decaying structures and dark waterways. Borcan Anabaptists explore places local families avoid, creating another disagreement about which inherited warnings deserve respect.

## Perugia and the Sforza

Perugia serves as a pilgrimage center and an approach to the western battlefield. Anabaptists seek Elysian blessings, Spitalians tend the wounded, and the Sforza’s workshops sharpen and supply weapons. Smithing stands at the center of the ruling family’s identity, with apprenticeship expected of its members. The city combines practical preparation with public declarations of faith, bringing together people whose confidence will soon be tested beyond its gates.

Returning fighters show the cost of those declarations. Maimed bodies and ruined equipment confront newcomers with consequences that sermons cannot soften. Fear can prevent departure, while returning without proof of battle threatens family honor. Rumors that the Sforza sell Psychonaut remains to deserters suggest an illicit route around those expectations. The allegations remain disputed, but investigation by Cathedral City reveals how reputation can become a resource to manipulate as well as a duty to uphold.

## Rival Families and Competing Loyalties

Macerata’s Modica gained Anabaptist influence, a network of informants, and a role in transmitting the city’s time to Cathedral City. Their alliance nevertheless cost them dearly in the Adriatic fighting. Succession now rests upon the young Celino after the deaths of his father and uncles under suspicious circumstances. In L’Aquila, the Capodieci pursue a more controlled relationship: Emilio Capodieci accepts the faith while limiting Cathedral City’s intervention and extending family influence beneath the broken cross.

Campobasso’s De Paulo reject that accommodation. Their resistance attacks Anabaptist representatives, destroys religious symbols, and receives protection from inhabitants who share their hostility to outside control. A locally minted Lira expresses economic independence alongside political defiance. These families offer different answers to the same question of authority, ranging from alliance and negotiation to assassination. Shared cultural roots do not prevent them from envisioning incompatible futures for Purgare.

## Santiago and Cruces

Santiago and Cruces provide Spitalian treatment for the casualties of the Adriatic conflict. Their hospitals make the Cult valuable to Purgan communities and Cathedral City, offering care where generations of fighting have left enduring injuries. Medical assistance also establishes a position from which the Spital can pursue its own strategic interests beyond the river.

The doctors seek access to the Balkhan and information about the Dushani, whose powers complicate an approach through Pollen. Apocalyptic and Jehammedan contacts help smuggle expeditions across the frontier, but local Clans and Voivodes obstruct their operations. Many infiltrators are captured before achieving their purpose. The relationship therefore joins sincere medical work to military preparation, making Purgan cooperation important to a campaign whose aims extend beyond Purgare itself.

## The Maw

South of the Alps, the Maw provides a rare approach to the volcanic depths of the Reaper’s Blow. A projecting plateau carries a path through hot, dry air toward a cliff overlooking basalt and broken rock. Altars and banners bearing family crests and broken crosses accompany travelers, marking generations of journeys into a place understood through religious obligation.

Anabaptists bring slain Psychonauts here and cast their bodies into the chasm. Tradition promises that humanity will defeat the Demiurge’s spawn when enough remains accumulate to form a crossing over the Blow. The scale makes fulfillment distant beyond any single life, yet the pilgrimage retains immediate meaning. Carrying a body to the edge connects a fighter’s service to a community and a task shared with generations who will never witness its completion.

## Towers and the Defense of Honor

Western families build thick-walled stone towers above the destructive conditions surrounding them. Shelter and defense make these buildings practical necessities, while their height announces a Clan’s strength and standing. A family unable to protect a tower can struggle to secure marriages, turning architecture into evidence of whether it deserves the trust of others.

Competition encourages towers to rise until ambition encounters the limits of the ground beneath them. In San Gimignano, an earthquake destroyed the upper works of competing structures and left only four families’ towers standing. Survivors interpreted the event as a sign and restrained further expansion. Elsewhere, building continues. The towers express both determination to endure and the danger of allowing reputation to demand more than stone, labor, or a damaged landscape can support.

## Harvesting Villages and Vigilantes

Some western villages survive beneath the protection of Psychokinetics. Force fields divert poisonous vapors and shelter productive slopes, allowing vineyards, olive groves, and daily life to persist in hostile country. The same beings release blood-feeding insects that return after feeding upon the inhabitants. Protection can become a system of dependence that gradually consumes the people it shelters.

Vigilantes defend their families against this parasitic relationship. They watch for altered light and insect activity, attempting to kill Psychokinetics before the beings retreat into protected Rifts. Short-range Luparas suit fighting where distorted forces divert distant shots. Their judgment carries weight with patriarchs and civic authorities, but their work leaves little expectation of a safe future. Protecting a Clan can require attacking the source of the shelter that made its village possible.

## Roma and Corpse

Roma’s inhabitants occupy catacombs and tunnels linking surviving buildings beneath the poisonous surface. Artworks, books, and other remnants of the past enter Neolibyan trade in exchange for weapons, wealth, and provisions. Shared feasts and crowded halls make the city a place of appetite and opportunity, where people use an extraordinary inheritance without necessarily seeking to preserve it. Travelers, traders, outlaws, and aspiring pirates gather within the same underground life.

The island of Corpse anchors piracy amid dangerous seas near the Reaper’s Blow. Captured hulls form coastal defenses, and wrecks complicate approaches already threatened by currents and volcanic disturbances. Roma’s trading connections help explain why pirates spare it while raiding African ships and settlements. Loot travels inland, including hunting rifles altered into Vigilantes’ Luparas. The weapons protecting western families can therefore arrive through commerce that joins local survival to violence at sea.

## Syracuse, Bedain

The Africans conquered fertile Sicily through bombardment and overwhelming military force. Consul Sarahali imposed the name Bedain and established authority beneath the Lion, making even the island’s identity part of its submission. Agricultural abundance mattered less to his ambitions than its position near the Purgan mainland and Balkhan, where ships could support expeditions across the Mediterranean.

Syracuse developed into a center for processing the proceeds of those expeditions. Ships unload recovered machinery, and Scrappers dismantle, repair, and sort it before useful goods return to African markets. Cranes, metal sheets, workshops, and salvage heaps transformed the city’s earlier character. The concentration offers Scrappers exceptional opportunity, while the authority organizing that opportunity rests upon conquest. Bedain connects Purgare’s productive resources and practical skills to an economy directed from beyond its shores.

## The Purgan Conflict

A Purgan character inherits relationships that make identity inseparable from responsibility. A family name can secure trust while carrying expectations about marriage, courage, faith, and obedience. Someone raised among eastern fields may regard Anabaptist commitment as the defense of everything worth preserving, while a western Vigilante or a resident of Campobasso understands independence through different threats. The people sharing a homeland do not necessarily agree about what threatens it most.

Purgare’s central struggle concerns who deserves authority over the lives a community protects. Family can offer belonging while enforcing a reputation at painful cost. Faith can supply food, healing, and resolve while drawing households into distant ambitions. Shelter can depend upon a being that feeds upon its inhabitants. For a Purgan, loyalty becomes meaningful through deciding which duties to honor, which demands to challenge, and whose future must matter when inherited obligations collide.
