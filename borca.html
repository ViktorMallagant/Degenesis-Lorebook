Culture & Society

Legacy of the Ancients

Borca rises from the physical remains of the Bygone world. Broken towers stand above fields of red dust, roots penetrate forgotten tunnels, and lichen spreads across concrete walls that once enclosed entire cities. Beneath those ruins lie machinery, metal, and artifacts capable of changing the fortunes of anyone who finds them. Generations of Borcans have learned to live among these remnants, recognizing opportunities in places others would see only as devastation.

Across the Culture, survival and reconstruction remain closely connected. Salvaged materials become bridges, workshops, weapons, and homes. Settlements grow into cities, and cities extend their authority over the surrounding land. Yet the people already living there have their own histories and claims. Borca’s future is being built upon an inheritance that no single Cult, city, or Clan can convincingly call its own.

A Homeland Divided

The Reaper’s Blow cuts Borca into western and eastern regions. Along this immense tectonic scar, shattered ground, volcanic activity, boiling water, and poisonous fumes make travel dangerous and settlement impossible across broad stretches. Rivers vanish into broken terrain, while the Alps provide difficult routes between lands that once belonged to a continuous landscape.

Shared cultural roots survive on both sides, but centuries of separation have encouraged different ways of living. Western Borca has developed a vigorous salvage economy supported by influential Cults and growing urban institutions. In the east, forests, herds, and local communities shape life more strongly, and the Chroniclers have never established the same commercial reach. These differences belong within one Culture whose people have inherited the same catastrophe under very different conditions.

Dust, Cold, and Adaptation

Northern West Borca lies beneath the pressure of advancing glaciers. Meltwater fills ancient channels and depressions, but salt in the soil limits the fertility that these waters seem to promise. Farther south, tundra gives way to grasslands, lakes, scrub, and ruined cities where birches and spruces grow in sheltered spaces. Gendos roam the abandoned streets, while Scrappers search the same terrain for remnants of the past.

Red dust remains one of the landscape’s defining features. Carried from impact craters, it gathers in urban canyons and penetrates clothing, buildings, and exposed machinery. Severe storms can injure eyes and lungs, making goggles, masks, scarves, and layers of heavy cloth essential protection. Vegetation increasingly binds the dust and storms have become less frequent, but the habits developed against them continue to shape Borcan life.

An Economy of Salvage

Every spring, large numbers of people leave the settlements to search the ruins. Marks scratched into concrete identify exhausted sites or warn travelers of danger. Useful scrap returns to the cities, while exceptional artifacts attract the Chroniclers’ attention. Metal is melted, repaired, traded, or stored until someone discovers a use for it. A successful expedition can support a workshop, repay a debt, or bring its finder into contact with people whose ambitions reach far beyond salvage.

This abundance makes metal part of everyday life. It reinforces doors, forms walkways, supports bridges, and supplies cooking vessels and decorations. Around western settlements, timber is comparatively scarce, with accessible forests already heavily exploited. The economy therefore rewards practical knowledge: knowing where to dig, how to recognize a valuable component, and how to turn the recovered material into something people need.

Justitian

At the center of western Borca’s political and commercial life stands Justitian. Its districts bring together Cults, artisans, laborers, traders, and people seeking safety beneath the Judges’ authority. Downtown combines crowded quarters, workshops, makeshift structures, religious communities, and Apocalyptic enterprises. Above it, Uptown concentrates the Judges’ institutions and the Chroniclers’ Central Cluster, reached from below by an engine-driven elevator platform.

Two great structures express the city’s industrial strength. Tech-Central houses Scrappers and Manufacturers whose workshops produce weapons, complex mechanisms, and extraordinary inventions. The Steel Monolith contains the Steel Masters’ forges, where the Judges’ muskets take shape. Across the city, Chronicler broadcasts and Judge patrols make the presence of authority difficult to ignore. Justitian offers opportunity and protection within a social order that carefully controls access to both.

The Price of Protection

The Protectorate grew as surrounding settlements accepted Justitian’s protection and submitted to its laws. Judgment stones and halls established the Judges’ presence beyond the capital, while the Codex gave communities a common framework for authority and punishment. Those who entered this system could gain security against enemies that an isolated village might never withstand.

Daily life also reveals its burdens. Providers cultivate fields, purchase water and fungicides, and depend upon Spitalian certification before their harvest can reach the market. Contaminated grain can erase a year’s labor, while fees and institutional demands steadily consume the rewards of successful work. The Providers’ Collective expresses the frustration of people whose labor sustains Justitian without giving them equal influence over its decisions. For others, punishment and compulsory labor demonstrate how quickly protection can become coercion.

The Return of the Clans

The Protectorate’s expansion displaced communities, destroyed homes, and denied Clans access to places carrying ancestral significance. Defeated groups preserved those injuries in their memories, even when they lacked the strength to act upon them. The fall of Praha Republika to Chernobog and his followers changed what resistance seemed capable of achieving. A supposedly impregnable city had been broken, and the example spread far beyond its ruins.

By 2595, Clan attacks threaten supply routes, patrols, and settlements across the Protectorate. The conflict reaches into the Black Lung itself, forcing Justitian to concentrate forces while exposed outposts struggle to hold their ground. Judges, Spitalians, Anabaptists, Chroniclers, and Jehammedans cooperate against the threat, with Scrappers providing information and Hellvetics protecting convoys. These alliances gather institutions with very different interests around the immediate need to keep the Protectorate standing.

The Spital and the Pursuit of Purity

Within the Black Lung’s southern ruins, the Spital forms another center of power. Patients gather in the Appendix, where treatment and accommodation are organized around their illnesses. Behind fortified boundaries, the Corpus contains laboratories and warehouses, while the Spital itself shelters medical knowledge and research inaccessible to most outsiders. Nearby Arnsberg houses the Preservists, whose military role extends the doctors’ influence beyond their hospitals.

Borca’s freedom from established Sepsis fields is maintained through constant vigilance and violent intervention. Spitalians inspect food, investigate infection, and pursue those who carry or spread contamination. Swarms arriving from Franka deepen suspicion of insects, prompting settlements to trap and destroy them for examination. Medical protection becomes inseparable from public fear: an inspection can preserve a community, but evidence of infection can also bring its destruction.

The Festering

Near Menden, a Spitalian assault destroyed a Mother spore field and unleashed a different catastrophe. Nanite cartridges transformed the surrounding environment into expanding black carbon structures, crystallized vegetation, and altered ruins. The resulting region became known as the Festering. Its growth slowed after the initial devastation, but never fully stopped, and strange patterns along its edges trouble the doctors who continue to observe it.

The Festering exposes the danger within Borca’s inheritance. Recovered technology can offer solutions beyond the capabilities of contemporary craftsmanship, yet the people using it may understand only part of what it can do. Here, a successful attack upon Sepsis left behind a landscape whose consequences remain unresolved.

Cathedral City and the Waterways

Cathedral City anchors Anabaptist influence in West Borca. Aqueducts carry water through its settlements and into a city of gardens, workshops, plantations, baptismal grounds, and monumental religious architecture. The Twin Spires dominate its skyline, while defended underground approaches control entry. Cultivation and faith meet in the effort to make damaged land productive again.

Water also connects Cathedral City to political struggles beyond its walls. Liqua grew around dependable underground reservoirs and became an important supplier to surrounding communities. Anabaptist involvement in its leadership secured influence over that resource, but renewed ties with Exalt challenged the arrangement. As Exalt’s people reclaim their settlement and uncover vast Bygone complexes, old relationships become the basis of new attempts at independence.

Routes and Refuge

The diminished Rain provides a route through western Borca even where its bed can no longer carry ships. Tracks, ruined piers, stranded vessels, and stretches of wild wheat trace its former course. Caravans and travelers follow these paths between settlements, although Clan tolls and attacks increasingly complicate passage. Noret, an overgrown city watched by mysterious guards, remains a particularly dangerous reminder that surviving ruins are not necessarily available for exploration.

Ferropol offers a different kind of refuge. Once a great forge city, it lost its Steel Masters to Justitian and became a place for those the Protectorate rejected or abandoned. Dust from the Wupper Crater repeatedly buries parts of the settlement, while iron-consuming Ferrites threaten its remaining material wealth. Nevertheless, Ferropol persists as a market where connections and money can obtain things unavailable within Justitian’s regulated order.

Through the Alps

The Hellvetics control crucial passages through the Alpine Fortress, linking western and eastern Borca and providing routes toward neighboring Cultures. Tunnels, bridges, strongholds, and gun emplacements allow movement across terrain that would otherwise demand extraordinary risk. Travelers queue for passage and pay for the protection of routes maintained by generations of soldiers.

These crossings make the Hellvetics essential to trade and communication. Their tolls also give geography a direct economic cost: moving goods between Borca’s halves requires access, money, and patience. For those unable or unwilling to pay, the mountains offer cold, unstable snow, hostile communities, and the possibility of encountering Hellvetic sharpshooters. Separation endures even where passage remains possible.

Beyond the Reaper’s Blow

Eastern Borca follows a different rhythm. Communities move with muskox herds or establish homes within pine forests, drawing sustenance and materials from the surrounding land. Timber is readily available, while metal is comparatively scarce. Without the Chroniclers’ western market system, recovered machinery has never generated the same widespread rush into the ruins.

Bygone cities remain hidden beneath moss, trees, and dense roots. Valuable artifacts still draw determined explorers, but retrieving and transporting them demands expensive journeys through the Alps. Local traditions and Clan loyalties retain considerable authority, making eastern Borca a place where knowledge of people and obligations matters as much as knowledge of roads. Urban life survives here too, alongside communities that have little reason to admire western ideas of reconstruction.

Osman and Praha

Osman stands as eastern Borca’s great urban center, strongly influenced by Jehammedan life and protected by its Janite warriors. Markets, baths, prosperous Clans, and an underground library express a confidence built around the city’s own institutions. The library’s connection to scholars from Praha reflects an eastern tradition of preserving knowledge that developed outside the Chroniclers’ western network.

Praha Republika’s defenses once excluded almost every outsider. Its destruction by Chernobog and the accompanying Clans shattered that reputation and released weapons and resources into the surrounding conflicts. Osman now confronts organized enemies, raids upon its herds, and escalating violence. Across the east, the fall of one powerful city threatens the security of another.

Contested Knowledge and Foreign Trade

Attempts to extend Chronicler influence eastward produced the Needle Towers. Fragments sent to occupy broadcast sites ultimately established powers of their own, drawing followers and mercenaries around their technological advantages. These towers became centers of trade, danger, and independent ambition, demonstrating how distance from a Cult’s central authority can transform its representatives.

Meanwhile, Neolibyan interest in Borcan artifacts threatens the Chroniclers’ western position. African merchants offer gifts, commercial opportunities, and alternative buyers, while their salvage crews enter territory the Chroniclers consider essential to their own plans. Competition over ruins becomes competition over relationships and access to knowledge. Borcans who gain new choices in trade may also find themselves caught between institutions unwilling to surrender their influence.

The Borcan Conflict

Borca’s people have built considerable strength from broken ground and salvaged materials. Cities offer safety, workshops restore useful knowledge, and communities preserve ways of living that survived the collapse of the old world. Each achievement also raises questions about ownership and authority. A fortified settlement needs supplies, a productive field needs water, and an extraordinary discovery can attract people powerful enough to take it.

A Borcan character may therefore carry pride in practical skill alongside suspicion of those who claim to represent civilization. Loyalty to a city can conflict with sympathy for displaced Clans; faith in medical protection can falter before the consequences of an inspection; a profitable find can challenge the obligations binding an explorer to family or Cult. Across both halves of the Culture, the struggle is to build a future while deciding what must be preserved, who should benefit, and whose claims deserve to be heard.
