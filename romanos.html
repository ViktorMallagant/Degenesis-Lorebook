CLAN DOSSIER

Romanos

The past is always for sale.

REGIONPurgare
TERRITORYRoma
TECH LEVELIII

KatharSys p. 243

BUSY

A Bazaar Built from Ruins

Ash and sulfur cover the lost coastal cities of western Purgare. Romanos enter abandoned houses and palaces, clearing paths to paintings, marble statues, mosaics, pillars, and gilded decorations. Treasures that once defined those cities are removed and taken to Roma.

There, a vast art market draws Neolibyan buyers through curtains and halls crowded with salvaged finery. The Clan profits from dismantling the cultural inheritance of its homeland and selling it to patrons who can afford to display the Bygones’ grandeur.

THE ETERNAL CITY

Nobles of Borrowed Splendor

Roma is divided among families whose leaders call themselves lords, barons, dukes, and kings. Rival domains can occupy neighboring corridors of the same monument. Mansions, museums, and tombs serve as courts, and old curtains become noble clothing.

Evocative titles and pageantry matter deeply to the Romanos, though much of their status is self-declared. Their attachment to power cannot disguise how much of their magnificence is borrowed from ruins.

Neolibyans embody the wealth and comfort the Romano families believe they deserve. Parents flatter their customers and seek places for their children in African retinues, hoping that service abroad will bring money and influence back to the household.

CUTTHROATS

Courtesy Has Its Limits

Theatrical friendliness ends when someone steals within a Romano domain. Wealth sustains a ready supply of hired criminals, and a word from a patron can make an intruder disappear into the alleys.

The pirates of Corpse are welcome business partners. For a suitable payment they will recover cargo that has already been sold, returning it to the bazaar for another transaction. Some statues have repeatedly traveled onto Neolibyan transports only to find their way back into the same sellers’ hands.
