WORLD OVERVIEW

Cults

Beliefs · Duties · Ambitions

Thirteen Cults, Thirteen Visions of Humanity

The Eshaton shattered the institutions of the old world, leaving survivors to rebuild their lives among its ruins. Over the following centuries, thirteen Cults emerged as powerful forces across Europe and Africa. Through medicine, technology, military discipline, commerce, faith, and ancestral tradition, they gave people ways to survive and purposes worth pursuing. Their influence now reaches from fortified cities and underground vaults to remote villages, caravan routes, and contested frontiers.

A Cult can be a scientific institution, a military order, a trading organization, a religious community, or a collection of people united by a shared way of life. Some preserve practices inherited from the Bygones. Others have developed beliefs and customs suited to a world their ancestors could scarcely have imagined. Each offers an understanding of humanity’s place in that world and a vision of what its future should become.

These visions frequently collide. The protection offered by one Cult can threaten the independence cherished by another. Knowledge can become a closely guarded resource, justice can justify conquest, and faith can turn disagreement into an unforgivable offense. The Cults sustain much of human civilization while also driving many of its fiercest conflicts.

Belonging and Obligation

Cult membership gives a person a place within a larger community. Training, equipment, shelter, employment, and trusted companions can make the difference between survival and an anonymous death in the wasteland. A recognized symbol can open a gate, secure an audience, or convince strangers that its bearer possesses valuable skills.

Those advantages carry obligations. Members answer to leaders, observe customs, perform duties, and accept restrictions upon their conduct. Some are recruited and trained; others inherit their place through family or grow up within communities where membership shapes their lives from childhood. Advancement can bring greater freedom and authority, but it also makes a person responsible for more than their own survival.

The relationship between member and Cult is seldom simple. A devoted healer may question an order to destroy an infected settlement. A soldier may discover that duty to the Fortress conflicts with loyalty to a comrade. A trader may have to choose between a profitable contract and a promise made to someone without money or influence. Personal convictions become meaningful when circumstances put them under pressure.

Power Across the Borders

Cultures provide roots in particular lands and communities. Cults often connect people across those boundaries, carrying practices, resources, and ambitions into distant regions. A shared affiliation can give travelers common ground despite differences in language or upbringing. It can also place them on the same side of a conflict neither fully understands.

Their influence reaches into ordinary life. Farmers depend upon trade routes and protection. Settlements need medicine, tools, and access to markets. Old machines acquire value because someone can repair them, decipher their purpose, or find a buyer. Religious ceremonies help communities understand suffering and preserve continuity between generations. Through these needs, Cults become part of everyday survival.

Their relationships combine cooperation with rivalry. Spitalians and Anabaptists confront the Primer through different understandings of the same enemy. Scrappers recover artifacts that Chroniclers seek to acquire. Neolibyans, Scourgers, and Anubians occupy complementary positions within African society, without always agreeing about its direction. An alliance can endure for generations while remaining full of resentment, competing interests, and unfinished arguments.

The Thirteen Cults

Anabaptists

Anabaptists seek to reclaim a world they understand as a corrupted Paradise. Their faith joins cultivation, healing, and communal labor to an uncompromising struggle against the Demiurge and its manifestations. Ascetics restore productive soil and prepare potent oils, while Orgiastics carry the fight to the enemies of their faith. They can bring food, purpose, and protection to struggling communities, with religious certainty shaping both their generosity and their violence.

Anubians

Anubians guide communities through healing, ritual, and the relationship between the living and their ancestors. Their teachings place individual transformation within a greater understanding of life, death, and the force known as Ka. They preserve traditions while pursuing knowledge through demanding paths of initiation. To outsiders, their authority can seem mysterious; within the communities they serve, it reaches into some of the most intimate and consequential moments of human life.

Apocalyptics

Apocalyptics gather in Flocks united by appetite, opportunity, and an intense engagement with the present. Gambling, entertainment, smuggling, sex, and intoxicants support networks that flourish wherever desire meets prohibition. Their bird names, Tarot, and internal hierarchies give structure to lives that can appear disorderly from outside. Companionship and freedom coexist with manipulation, dependency, and exploitation, making a Flock both a refuge and a dangerous place to belong.

Chroniclers

The Chroniclers gather information, recover Bygone technology, and pursue the restoration of the Stream. Their masked figures and sophisticated equipment cultivate an imposing reputation, while their networks give them influence far beyond their physical presence. Knowledge is their great resource: collected, classified, traded, and carefully controlled. To encounter a Chronicler is to meet someone who may know much more about a situation than they are willing to reveal.

Clanners

Clanners encompass countless communities with distinct ancestors, customs, beliefs, and ambitions. Farmers, hunters, herders, craftspeople, and warriors preserve ways of life that have sustained humanity through centuries of upheaval. Some Clans wander; others defend settled homes or sophisticated traditions. Their loyalties usually begin with their own people. Understanding a Clanner therefore requires understanding the particular Clan that raised them and the obligations it places upon its members.

Hellvetics

From their Alpine Fortress, the Hellvetics preserve a military tradition built upon discipline, preparedness, and loyalty to comrades. They guard mountain passages, maintain formidable defenses, and regard their weapons and supplies as resources entrusted to them for the Fortress’s survival. Their strength rests upon coordinated action and an enduring sense of duty. Beyond their defenses, a changing world increasingly challenges the assumptions behind their orders.

Jehammedans

Jehammedan society centers upon family, inherited duty, and the teachings of Jehammed. A person’s position within the community shapes their responsibilities, expectations, and opportunities to earn recognition. Faith gives endurance and meaning to lives bound closely to kin, while honor and sacrifice connect individual conduct to the family’s standing. Those bonds can sustain people through immense hardship, even as they impose limits upon the futures they are permitted to imagine.

Judges

The Judges carry the authority of Justitian and its Codex into the Protectorate. They pursue criminals, settle disputes, defend settlements, and enforce a vision of civilization founded upon law. Their presence can offer security where violence once determined every outcome. It can also bring demands that independent communities refuse to accept. Between the written law and conditions in the wasteland, individual Judges must decide how justice can actually be delivered.

Neolibyans

Neolibyans extend their influence through trade, investment, exploration, and the pursuit of opportunity. Ships, caravans, plantations, and expeditions connect their commercial ambitions to communities across Africa and Europe. Wealth can support remarkable achievements and open routes others consider impossible. It also gives them power over people whose choices are constrained by debt, dependence, or slavery. Their confidence in progress invites the question of who shares in its rewards.

Palers

Palers descend from communities that endured underground, preserving their vaults and awaiting the awakening of those they revere as divine. Generations in darkness have shaped their bodies, senses, customs, and understanding of the surface. As their bunkers open, necessity and inherited expectations draw them into a world that has developed without them. Their encounters with its inhabitants carry the weight of ancient promises and a profound separation between two human experiences.

Scourgers

Scourgers uphold an African warrior tradition shaped by ancestral memory, physical excellence, and a demanding hierarchy. Their packs combine distinct battlefield roles into disciplined forces capable of protecting trade and carrying war into foreign lands. They understand their strength as service to their people and their ancestors. In Europe, that conviction accompanies conquest, vengeance, and enslavement, giving their campaigns consequences that extend far beyond the battlefield.

Scrappers

Scrappers search the remains of the Bygone world for salvage, machinery, and artifacts. Their work demands endurance, practical knowledge, and an intimate understanding of dangerous ruins. Recovered materials support workshops and settlements, while extraordinary finds attract buyers with resources and ambitions of their own. Scrappers stand between a vanished civilization and the people rebuilding from its remains, risking their lives for discoveries whose full significance may escape them.

Spitalians

Doctors, researchers, and warriors, the Spitalians confront disease and the expanding influence of the Primer. Their hospitals and medical knowledge make them indispensable to communities across Europe, while their campaigns against Sepsis demand strict discipline and ruthless decisions. They understand their work as the defense of humanity itself. For those who depend upon them, the promise of healing exists alongside the fear of inspection, quarantine, and destruction.

People Within the Symbols

A Cult’s public reputation can conceal considerable variety among its members. Leaders and recruits face different pressures. Specialists may disagree about methods, and communities far from a Cult’s center may adapt its practices to local conditions. Shared symbols do not guarantee identical motives, equal knowledge, or unquestioning obedience.

For characters, these differences create relationships worth exploring. A mentor may embody everything someone admires about their Cult. A superior may demand something they cannot accept. A rival may serve the same institution while pursuing a very different future for it. Even cooperation between hostile Cults becomes possible when particular people find reasons to trust one another.

The Cults give Degenesis much of its political and social structure. Their members bring that structure to life through work, ambition, conviction, compromise, and the decisions they make when those commitments collide.

Mechanical Summary

Choose a Cult as part of character creation, alongside your Culture and Concept. Your character has joined that Cult or was born into it, placing them within a social framework of beliefs, customs, duties, and hierarchy. Consider how they became a member, who trained them, and what they hope to achieve through that affiliation. These details help turn an organization on the character sheet into people and responsibilities that matter during play.

Each Cult lists five Skills whose character creation maximums receive a +1 MAX increase, as shown in KatharSys p. 41. These increases allow you to spend more of your available Skill points on the listed Skills; they do not grant free points. Bonuses from Culture, Concept, and Cult combine when they apply to the same Skill. You may use those higher limits where they suit your character without being required to reach them.

Your starting rank depends upon the requirements you can satisfy. After allocating your traits and Backgrounds, follow your Cult’s rank progression and check the prerequisites for advancement. A character can begin above the first rank when their choices meet the necessary conditions. Record the equipment and benefits associated with that progression, and consider the duties accompanying the position.

Cult membership also provides access to Cult-specific Potentials and equipment, subject to their requirements and the limits of your rank. During character creation, choose a qualifying Cult-specific or Common Potential at level 1. Consult your Cult’s rank information when determining which resources are available to you.

For Clanners, define the particular Clan to which your character belongs. You can choose an established Clan or create one with your GM. The standard Clanner Skill bonuses describe a life shaped by survival and conflict; with the GM’s approval, a Clan with a different way of life can use five alternative Skills appropriate to its traditions.

Together, Culture, Concept, and Cult establish your character’s origins, outlook, and place within society. Let Cult membership inform what they know, whom they answer to, and what others expect from them. Their response to those expectations will help determine the person they become.
