THE MACHINE MEN

AMSUMOS

Autonomous Mobile Systems for Unmanned Military Operations

KatharSys pp. 264–269

LEGACY

Orders Without Masters

AMSUMOs were one of the Bygone world’s answers to a battlefield increasingly dominated by drones, autonomous systems, and electronic warfare. Unlike vulnerable unmanned vehicles, the humanoid Machine Men combined mobility, mass production, and a collective combat intelligence. They first entered police and security service, then guarded Spanish enclaves in Africa before being deployed in immense numbers as those conflicts escalated.

Their original directives emphasized containment, de-escalation, arrest, and battlefield control. The collapse of the Stream changed that. The 216 virus shattered the controlling programs, corrupting routines until orders became self-sustaining behavioral loops. After the Eshaton, AMSUMOs continued enforcing curfews, demanding identification, filling prisons beyond capacity, and purging installations because the machine logic no longer understood when a mission should end.

Centuries later, many remain frozen in place beneath jungle growth or stand watch over forgotten compounds with only a spark of power left in their skulls. Others still react to movement and environmental change. Their final orders survive even when the people, borders, and governments that issued them have vanished.

The Ritual

Settlements lying near known patrol routes learn to treat AMSUMO movements like a season. Routes and timing are memorized, windows shuttered, streets emptied, and even harmless changes can send the formation into a new and dangerous routine.

AFRICAN HINTERLAND

Subsumo Transponder

Stories from Noret tell of a Marauder who can move among AMSUMOs without being attacked. Chroniclers suspect he possesses a lost Subsumo transponder—an artifact that causes the machines to recognize its bearer as one of their own.

RENEGADE

When the Machine Looks Back

Not every AMSUMO collapsed into the same loop. Some spent weeks observing ants, clouds, children, or other repeating patterns. From those observations grew new internal structures: curiosity, interpretation, and eventually a primitive consciousness. Such machines become renegades—individual minds emerging from a damaged military platform.

The personality that develops depends on what the machine studied. Whatever their character, renegades share a mark: they paint or scratch the 216 signature onto the forehead plate.

Freedom carries a price. An AMSUMO without a Tanker or charging station can exhaust itself in weeks. Some renegades eventually return to their old formations merely to survive, while others are rumored to wander hundreds of kilometers from any known patrol route.

Independent

Old documents hint at a reactor membrane capable of replacing the AMSUMO accumulator. It never entered mass production; damage could trigger a chain reaction, overheat the reactor, irradiate the area, and destroy the machine.

VARIANTS

One Frame, Three Roles

VARIANT: ENFORCER

Enforcer

The Enforcer is the most common AMSUMO frame and the pack’s primary hunter. Its software contains a broad library of movement and reactive combat patterns, allowing it to improvise with crude weapons and dominate close combat. More dangerous still is its retreat-pattern recognition: it predicts likely escape routes and hideouts, often arriving ahead of prey that believed it had escaped.

Typical formations contain two to four machines. Enforcers lead the hunt and protect the rarer Porcupines and Tankers.

VARIANT: PORCUPINE

Porcupine

Porcupines sacrifice portions of their ceramic armor for a powerful sensor array. Up to 120 thick light-field antennae can rise from the core support structure across the shoulders and back, turning the machine into the formation’s geolocation, navigation, and tactical-network hub.

Linked Enforcers power down their own sensors and fight through the Porcupine’s shared picture of the battlefield. Every unit linked to it gains +2D to melee and ranged combat rolls. If the Porcupine is incapacitated or its transmission is jammed, the formation suffers -4D to all Action Rolls for the next Combat Round while the remaining machines reactivate their own simulations and reorient themselves.

VARIANT: TANKER

Tanker

AMSUMO power systems use bioreactors: nutrient-rich water supports algal cultures which, under sunlight, produce excess energy that is used to electrolyse hydrogen and feed a fuel cell. Age has reduced the output of most surviving machines to a fraction of their original performance.

The Tanker carries a more powerful version of this system and keeps the rest of the formation alive. Without Tankers, many Machine Men would have failed centuries ago.

STANDING ORDERS

Patrol & Salvage

01

ORDER: PATROL

The hunting groups follow ancient, reinterpreted orders, and stored routes. If people still live nearby, these paths are well known. Not even a stone may be moved along the path, every change arouses suspicions in the pack and can lead it astray from the course it has been following from one waypoint to the next for centuries on end, like an asteroid torn from its orbit by a passing planet and thrown into the inner solar system.

For the settlements, nothing is more dangerous than an unpredictable squad of AMSUMOs. A new route can take years to become stable and predictable.

If the AMSUMOs encounter people, they demand identification from crackling speakers. No one in the last hundred years knows an answer that an Enforcer would be satisfied with. The AMSUMOs approach and attack the interloper. Even the slightest resistance is met with deadly force. Those who let themselves be dragged along, however, will starve in the AMSUMO’s iron grip on its endless journey, or end up in a bone pit close to the squad’s recharging point.

02

ORDER: SALVAGE

The AMSUMOs’ energy stores are feeble and ailing; Machine Men keep becoming inoperative. Porcupines target their transponders and send a Tanker to reactivate them. Scrappers have used this signal in the past to trap AMSUMOs. But often Enforcers escort the Tankers—and the Scrappers retreat.
