CLAN DOSSIER

Adriani

One family. Two faiths.

REGIONPurgare / Balkhan
TERRITORYAdriatic Basin
TECH LEVELIII

KatharSys p. 241

HOME

Across the Adriatic

Caught between Anabaptist and Jehammedan armies, the settlers of the Adriatic basin traded farming for war. Many joined the Anabaptists, who welcomed them, armed them, and sent them back into the conflict. Years of fighting consumed their children and the lives they had hoped to build.

Eventually, more than four hundred Anabaptists abandoned their camp at night. Leaving armor and Spitfires behind, they crossed the Adriatic with swords in hand, approached a Jehammedan camp, and surrendered. Their defection was an attempt to end the struggle that had made enemies of people they considered good.

FAMILY

A New Clan

The defectors set aside their separate family identities and became the Adriani. They cultivated land and bought goats from the Jehammedans. Suspicion remained, but hostility gave way to evenings spent drinking tea together. Outcast Delilas found a home among the newcomers.

Jehammedan family customs became a model for the Clan. Sons worked the fields and supported their parents before establishing households of their own. Daughters were raised apart from the boys and prepared for marriage and labor. Those expectations also planted the first seeds of rebellion.

RETURN

The Price of Belonging

The elders now seek acceptance through marriage between the two peoples. Adriani daughters are brought to Jehammedan camps as prospective brides, while sons must approach an Abrami to ask for a Hagari’s hand. To the old generation, these unions promise the fulfillment of their ancestors’ hopes.

Younger Adrianis increasingly resent being offered as the price of that dream. Their parents’ desire to become Jehammedan in spirit has become another demand imposed on their future, and many look back toward the faith their ancestors abandoned.

THREE DOTS

An Inheritance Reclaimed

Fading Anabaptist tattoos still mark the elders’ foreheads. For their children, the same three dots represent redemption. Using sharpened goat bones and soot, young Adrianis tattoo themselves in imitation of the Anabaptists.

Parents answer with beatings, but the children increasingly resist. A Clan founded by people who crossed a battlefield to escape religious division now finds that division reopening inside its own families.
