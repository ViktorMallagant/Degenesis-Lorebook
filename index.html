Alpha & Omega

THE WORLD AFTER 2073

Eshaton

In 2073, the old world ended in a single night. Asteroids tore into the planet from Scandinavia through the Alps, Italy, and the African coast, rupturing the Earth’s crust and unleashing earthquakes, tsunamis, magma, and storms of stone. Cities disappeared beneath fire and ash. Communications died. By morning, volcanic dust and crater debris had swallowed the sky, reducing the sun to a dim light behind a poisonous red haze.

Ten thousand years of civilization collapsed almost overnight. Yet humanity did not disappear with it. Those who survived searched for meaning in what had happened and found something beyond simple apocalypse. The end of the old world could also be the beginning of another.

They called it the Eshaton.

Ice Age

Fire gave way to darkness, and darkness gave way to cold.

Ash and dust remained suspended across the atmosphere until snow began to mingle with the poisonous rain. Temperatures plunged, glaciers expanded, and the polar ice caps drew water from the oceans. Northern Europe disappeared beneath snow and ice while survivors retreated underground, emerging only to scavenge fuel and supplies. As sea levels fell, former harbors stood stranded above vast stretches of exposed seabed.

Africa followed another path. Atmospheric currents diverted much of the dust away from its northern and central regions. Rain swept inland from the Atlantic, temperatures moderated, and new subtropical forests spread across lands once dominated by desert.

While Europe froze, the Sahara blossomed.

Resurrection

Eventually, the sky began to clear. Rain washed the volcanic dust from the atmosphere, sunlight returned, and the ice cracked beneath warmer summers. Streams began flowing again. Grass crept across the red plains. New growth emerged from blackened forests.

Humanity followed.

Survivors crossed the wastelands searching for food, fuel, and somewhere to begin again. Scarcity produced brutality as easily as cooperation; Clans fought settlements and one another for the remnants of a shattered world. Yet fortified communities endured. Knowledge survived, was rediscovered, and slowly became useful again.

Over the following centuries, civilization rebuilt itself into something entirely new. Thirteen Cults gave structure and purpose to humanity, while seven Cultures emerged across Europe and Africa. Cities such as Justitian and Tripol flourished, and ships once again carried people and goods across the Mediterranean.

By 2595, humanity has survived the Eshaton.

That does not mean it has survived what came with it.

Infected

The asteroids carried more than destruction.

Locked within their strange matter was the Primer, an alien agent capable of altering life at its most fundamental level. Years after the impacts, it began escaping from the craters as a black mist. Microscopic spores penetrated organic matter and wrapped themselves around the genetic structures of their hosts, not simply mutating them but opening entirely new biological pathways.

Humanity was among the organisms it found.

From that process emerged Homo Degenesis: beings better adapted to the transformed world and capable of phenomena that seemed psychic or supernatural. Around the craters they developed into creatures radically removed from ordinary humanity, linked by forces that suggested not individuals acting alone, but fragments of something larger.

The Eshaton had not merely destroyed the world. It had introduced a competitor for it.

Sepsis

Near the impact craters, the Primer found another vessel: fungus.

What first appeared to survivors as the Earth itself beginning to rot became known as Sepsis. Beneath the ground, fungal mycelia spread outward from the craters in expanding circles, consuming nutrients until the surface swelled and ruptured with pale fungal growth.

Those blooms matured rapidly, hardened, and finally tore themselves apart, releasing clouds of spores into the air.

Then the cycle began again.

Each generation exhausted another layer of earth and pushed farther from the crater. Over decades, the resulting spore fields carved concentric ridges and valleys across the landscape, expanding like ripples from a stone dropped into water.

The infection was no longer confined to the places where the asteroids had fallen. It was spreading.

Mother Spore Fields

Some spore fields survived cycle after cycle until they became immense structures kilometers across.

Their concentric rings grew deeper and higher as new layers formed over the old, while pale veils of Sepsis hung above them in the wind. Eventually another phenomenon appeared: magnetic distortion. Compass needles began turning toward the field's center.

At that point, an ordinary spore field had become something greater.

A Mother spore field.

These vast organisms are more than outbreaks of alien fungus. They are centers from which the Primer's influence spreads into the surrounding world.

And they produce something humanity willingly carries far beyond their borders.

Burn

Mother spore fields bloom with thick-skinned purple cusps whose contents can be harvested without immediately bursting.

Inside is Burn.

Inhaled or ingested, its spores produce an experience so powerful that users describe cascading colors, overwhelming emotion, and freedom from the body's ordinary limitations. Hunger becomes distant. Cold becomes tolerable. The ruined world becomes easier to endure.

That makes Burn valuable.

It also makes it one of the Primer's most effective weapons.

Across Europe and Africa, people harvest it, sell it, smuggle it, and consume it. What feels like escape is also infection.

Phenotype

Burn leaves its mark.

Once inside the bloodstream, the infection spreads outward through the body. Fine veins emerge beneath the skin, developing into circular rashes that eventually form complex symbols.

Stranger still, those symbols are not identical.

People originating from different regions develop different marks. Borcans, Frankans, Pollners, Balkhani, Purgans, and Africans each manifest patterns connected to the environment in which they developed—even when no obvious genetic explanation exists.

The implication is disturbing. The Primer does not appear to react only to inherited DNA. It responds to the phenotype: the organism shaped by its environment, its development, and perhaps the land itself.

The different Cultures of the new world may be written into humanity more deeply than anyone understands.

HOMO

DEGENESIS

The Seed

For some Burn users, intoxication ends.

The infection does not.

Spores continue growing inside their bodies until fungal matter spreads around the mouth and throat. They cough flakes and contaminated fluids wherever they travel, seeding new outbreaks of Sepsis in the places where they sleep and live.

These infected humans become Leperos—walking vectors through which the Primer carries itself into new territory.

What humanity consumes as a drug eventually learns to use humanity as transportation.

Epigenesis

Adults infected by Sepsis are consumed.

Unborn children are changed.

Pregnancies near Mother spore fields can produce children whose developing biology responds to the Primer instead of simply being destroyed by it. These children—called Soulless Ones, Aberrants, or Psychonauts—grow increasingly unlike ordinary humans.

Their transformations differ by region. In Pollen, bodies develop armor-like bone growths. In Franka, glands produce pheromones capable of influencing those around them. Balkhani Aberrants intrude upon the human mind through sound. Hybrispanian children manifest phenomena connected to time and perception. In Purgare, even gravity and matter seem to respond to them.

Humanity experiences Sepsis as disease. For the Psychonauts, it is development.

Portents and Wonders

As Psychonautic abilities manifest, ancient symbols appear around them—symbols humanity has associated with the body's Chakras for thousands of years.

Seven such energy centers lie along the human body's central axis. In ordinary traditions they are meant to exist in balance.

Within a Psychonaut, only one dominates.

That singular Chakra becomes the defining center of the Aberrant's existence, shaping both its abilities and the direction of its transformation. The others fade into insignificance while one aspect grows ever more extreme.

What ancient humanity described through mysticism now appears within Homo Degenesis as something horrifyingly real.

The Final Battle

Mother spore fields continue to rise across the world, transforming entire regions in their image. Within them, Psychonauts flourish—creatures increasingly adapted to an environment the Primer itself is creating.

Where those domains overlap with human settlements, more Aberrants are born.

The struggle is therefore no longer simply against famine, ruined cities, hostile Clans, or the scars of the Eshaton. Humanity is confronted by another form of life competing for the same world—and perhaps attempting to replace it.

The asteroid strikes were only the beginning.

Humanity survived the Eshaton.
Now it must survive Degenesis.
