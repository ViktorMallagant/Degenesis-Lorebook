Briefing

The Last Bastion

The Spitalians consider themselves humanity's last line of defense against the Primer and everything it has created. Psychonauts, spore fields, Sepsis, Burn, and the mutations spreading across Europe are all symptoms of the same greater threat. Their war is fought beneath microscopes as often as with Splayers and fungicide rifles. Tissue is dissected, genomes are studied, Psychonautic phenomena are catalogued, and new compounds are developed in the hope that every discovery brings humanity closer to defeating the Primer.

Medicine and warfare therefore exist side by side. Spitalians treat disease, perform surgery, vaccinate settlements, explore contaminated regions, retrieve samples, and study the Sepsis. When treatment is no longer possible, Famulancers and Preservists carry that same knowledge into battle.

Origins

The Spital grew from the Southern Ruhr Crisis Center, a medical complex overwhelmed after the Eshaton. Refugees flooded toward it while doctors struggled against disease, violence, shortages, and social collapse. Looters eventually stormed the facility, but former UEO personnel helped restore order and the Crisis Center gradually became the Spital.

When HIVE spread through the Ruhr region, the doctors sealed the gates rather than risk admitting the disease. Refugees and patients were turned away while the epidemic consumed them. The memory of that failure still shapes the Cult's obsession with contamination and hygiene.

Purity and Medicine

Purity is life. Spitalians shave their bodies, disinfect themselves, inspect food and living quarters, enforce hygiene laws, and subject those entering sensitive areas to rigorous purification. Hygienists carry this doctrine into the field, certifying food and rooms, maintaining disinfection facilities, attacking spore fields, hunting Leperos, and ordering de-sporeing where their authority is recognized.

For all their militarism, the Spitalians remain physicians. Field Medics diagnose and stabilize patients, Surgeons perform complex operations, Anesthesiologists manage intensive care and pain, Pharmacists maintain drugs and develop new compounds, and Epigeneticists investigate how the Primer alters human biology. Village Doctors may spend years serving remote communities.

Treatment is not always charity. The Spital consumes enormous quantities of food, energy, medicine, manpower, and equipment, so patients and communities may repay its services with Drafts, supplies, labor, oil, or political support.

The War Against the Primer

Primer knowledge permeates Spitalian education. Doctors study the Raptures, spore fields, Psychonautic mutations, Chakra activity, and the behavior of the Sepsis. Captured specimens may be examined, dead Psychonauts dissected, and biological samples carried back to laboratories searching for weaknesses in an enemy that continually adapts.

Their tools reflect this approach. Mollusks detect creatures linked to the Earth Chakras, while Noumenon Vocalizers identify and locate Psychonautic activity. SP 4016 TH destroys Sepsis, EX-Aerosol temporarily interferes with Psychonautic abilities, and Fungicide Rifles or Cartridge Launchers deliver chemical agents.

Success is rarely permanent. Spore fields adapt, chemicals lose effectiveness, new organisms appear, and old assumptions collapse under new evidence. Spitalian science exists in a permanent race against an enemy whose greatest weapon may be evolution itself.

Leperos and the Limits of Mercy

Spore infestation does not immediately mean a person is beyond saving. Protective equipment can prevent exposure, mental resistance can slow the Primer's influence, and EX can suppress infestation under the right conditions.

A threshold eventually exists beyond which the Spitalians believe there is nothing left to recover. Leperos still possess human bodies, histories, families, and memories, but the Sepsis has bound them permanently to the Primer. Their behavior becomes intertwined with the Chakra collective, and EX eventually ceases to offer a lasting solution.

When treatment fails completely, death becomes the remaining answer. Entire Leperos communities may be burned or destroyed with chemical agents rather than allowed to spread the Sepsis farther. Spitalians see such acts as desperate amputations performed upon humanity itself. Many outsiders see considerably less distinction between necessity and atrocity.

Hierarchy and Authority

Spitalian advancement begins with education. Recruits perform menial duties while learning to read and understand surviving medical knowledge. Orderlies master basic treatment and triage. Famulancers become full medical students and form much of the Cult's visible rank and file.

From there, careers divide into medical and specialist paths. Registrars oversee doctors and act for the Consultants, while eight Consultants stand at the summit of the ordinary hierarchy, setting directives, guiding research, and controlling restricted knowledge.

Hippocrats serve as diplomats, investigators, and the Spital's institutional conscience. They investigate complaints, monitor research, enforce ethical codes, and can seal laboratories or remove doctors from office. Spitalian morality can be severe, but the Cult possesses mechanisms intended to restrain its own excesses.

The Preservists

Preservists stand apart from the normal medical hierarchy. Chosen after exceptional service against the Spital's enemies, they train at Arnsberg to become the Cult's elite warriors. They lead Famulancers into contaminated territory, destroy Psychonauts, purge Leperos settlements, and attack spore fields where medicine alone is no longer enough.

Provost Kranzler commands the Corps through his Commando Primes. Officially subordinate to the Consultants, he nevertheless holds an unusually independent position and can veto decisions concerning the Spital's security.

Knowledge, Secrecy, and Heresy

Scientific knowledge is sacred, but access to it is tightly controlled. Research groups guard discoveries, Consultants restrict archives, and specialized laboratories pursue work ordinary Famulancers may never know exists.

Dr. Hernez Vasco became one of the Cult's most infamous heretics after arguing that the Primer might be more deeply connected to terrestrial life than accepted doctrine allowed and suggesting it might hold new possibilities for humanity. His case exposes an enduring contradiction: science demands that evidence overturn old assumptions, yet the Spital's identity depends upon the Primer remaining the enemy. Dangerous discoveries can therefore become political threats as quickly as scientific breakthroughs.

Humanity's Saviors

Across Europe, Spitalians are needed, respected, feared, and hated. Farmers need vaccines. Settlements need doctors. Armies need Field Medics. Communities threatened by spore fields may have no one else capable of recognizing the danger before catastrophe follows.

Anabaptists are their closest natural allies in the war against the Sepsis despite radically different beliefs. Relations with other Cults are usually pragmatic, while Burn smugglers and those knowingly spreading the Sepsis can expect little tolerance.

Reliance does not erase fear. Spitalians may arrive carrying medicine for an epidemic and return later with Preservists prepared to burn the same settlement if contamination has progressed too far.

The Spitalian Conflict

Spitalians are not emotionless scientists or uniformly cruel fanatics. They can be compassionate physicians, exhausted students, ambitious researchers, ruthless Preservists, devoted Village Doctors, political Hippocrats, or people who simply believe their training gives them a responsibility no one else is equipped to bear.

Their defining conflict lies in the impossible choices created by that responsibility. A doctor is trained to preserve life, yet a Spitalian must recognize when saving one life may endanger thousands. Scientific curiosity encourages investigation of the Primer, while survival demands its destruction.

Every Spitalian must eventually decide where healing ends and the war begins. That boundary is rarely as clear as the Spital would like it to be.
